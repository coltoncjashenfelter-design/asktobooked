Anonymized findings from audits run on real businesses in cities around the country. Names withheld — the gaps are the point.

Plumbing Spokane, WA

We asked AI the four highest-value questions a homeowner actually types — burst pipe, flooded basement, no hot water, water-heater replacement. Every answer went to a national franchise or an out-of-state lead-gen site. The city's established shops, some with 20–50 years in town, surfaced only in the generic "best plumber" list. Not once for an emergency.

Estimated exposure for one local shop missing these calls: $25,000–$80,000/yr

HVAC Toledo, OH

One local company has run since the 1960s and gets named for "best HVAC" and "furnace replacement." But ask AI who to call for emergency AC repair — the call that becomes a $7,000–$18,000 system replacement — and it's gone. Two competitors that show up in every query took those answers instead.

Estimated exposure: $30,000–$100,000+/yr

Roofing Boise, ID

The metro's most-reviewed roofers — one with 795 reviews, one with 50 years in business — are named for "best roofer" and even "storm damage." Ask AI for emergency roof-leak repair, though, and it returns national lead-generation sites instead. The active-leak call that becomes a full replacement gets routed out of state.

Estimated exposure: $25,000–$75,000+/yr

How we run these: we ask the AI assistants people actually use the same questions your customers do — "best plumber in [city]," "burst pipe, who do I call" — and record who gets named and who doesn't. Exposure is an estimate: the high-intent calls a business misses, times typical local ticket values — emergency plumbing runs $250–$800 a call, a new HVAC system $7,000–$18,000, a roof replacement $8,000–$16,000. Findings are real and current; the businesses stay anonymous.

About asktobooked

It started with a car repair.

Before asktobooked was a company, it was a question. While researching local mechanics, we watched an AI tool answer with one confident name — and wondered what made that shop "better" than the ten others within five miles of it. The uncomfortable answer: nothing. It was simply the one the machines could read.

That question became a testing rig, and the rig became a practice: asking AI platforms the exact questions customers ask — emergency calls, comparisons, "who do you recommend" — in cities across the country, then tracking which businesses get named and reverse-engineering why. The pattern is remarkably consistent. And most of what decides it can be fixed in an afternoon.

The field guide is that practice written down in plain language. The consulting is us running it for you.
